## Voice Summary

Jeff Hill Trailer Sales speaks like the owner — because it literally is the owner. The voice is first-person, casual, and passion-driven: a guy who loves racing, knows trailers cold, and would rather shake your hand than hand you a brochure. There's no corporate layer here. The copy is plain-spoken and direct, heavy on exclamation marks and light on jargon. Trust is established through honesty ("no hidden fees, no pushy salesmen") rather than credentials or polish. The brand's tone lives somewhere between friendly small-town mechanic and knowledgeable racing buddy — someone who will actually help you get what you need.

## Tone Spectrum

- **Formal ←——→ Casual:** Far casual. First-person "I/me/my," contractions assumed, direct address ("Let me help you"). No corporate language anywhere.
- **Serious ←——→ Playful:** Leans slightly playful — "DREAM TRAILER," "Join the family," racing metaphors ("race into Jeff Hill Trailer Sales") — but always purposeful, never frivolous.
- **Authoritative ←——→ Peer-to-peer:** Peer-to-peer with expertise. Jeff speaks as someone who has raced, lived in trailers, and knows the community — not as a salesperson lecturing down.
- **Reserved ←——→ Expressive:** Expressive. Heavy use of capitalization for emphasis, exclamation points, and strong affirmative statements. Emotions visible.
- **Technical ←——→ Accessible:** Balanced. Product specs appear in detail (GVW, axle ratings, interior dimensions) when selling, but brand copy stays simple and accessible. Technical depth is available but not the opening pitch.

## Voice Pillars

**1. Straight Talk**
Jeff Hill's brand is built on being the anti-dealer. The most prominent non-tagline message on the site is verbatim: "NO HIDDEN FEES OR PUSHY SALESMEN." This directness runs through everything — plain CTAs ("CALL TODAY," "GET YOUR QUOTE TODAY"), no marketing fluff, no corporate hedging.
- *Example from source:* "NO HIDDEN FEES OR PUSHY SALESMEN. COME CHECK US OUT AT JEFF HILL TRAILER SALES."
- *How to apply:* Every piece of content should say what it means. No filler phrases. No vague superlatives. If it can't be said simply, it shouldn't be said.

**2. Personal, Owner-Driven Voice**
Jeff speaks in first person as himself, not as a brand. "I started selling trailers," "Let me help you," "my extreme knowledge." This is a person brand wrapped in a business name.
- *Example from source:* "Let me help you personally custom design your trailer to fit your needs and budget!"
- *How to apply:* Write as Jeff, not as "we." Where brand copy is required, lean into the owner story. Never abstract the voice into a faceless company.

**3. Racing Passion as Proof**
Jeff's racing background is not an anecdote — it's the credential. It explains why his trailer knowledge is different from a typical dealer's. The word "extreme" appears in: "my extreme knowledge of racing and trailers."
- *Example from source:* "In 2012 I started Racing Karts in Indiana... I knew what it was like to work, live, and travel in a trailer."
- *How to apply:* Tie expertise claims back to racing experience. Don't just say "we know trailers" — say why, specifically. The racing community is the home base audience.

**4. Family / Community First**
The brand frames buyers as joining something, not just buying something. "Family" is the operative word. Facebook Live, community engagement, and personal phone numbers all support this.
- *Example from source:* "Join the Jeff Hill Trailer Sales family today."
- *How to apply:* Use inclusive, belonging language. Reference the community. Avoid transactional language ("purchase," "sale," "order") in favor of relationship language ("let's build," "join us," "talk trailers").

**5. Dream + Practical in One**
Jeff simultaneously sells aspiration ("DREAM TRAILER") and practicality ("fit your needs and budget"). This is deliberate — he serves both hobbyists dreaming big and small business owners watching every dollar.
- *Example from source:* "Let my extreme knowledge of racing and trailers help you build your 'DREAM TRAILER' to fit your needs and budget."
- *How to apply:* Don't pick a lane between aspirational and practical — hold both. Lead with the dream, follow with the budget reality.

## Vocabulary Guide

### Words and Phrases to Use
- "Let me help you" / "Let me personally"
- "Family"
- "Dream trailer"
- "Fit your needs and budget"
- "Let's talk trailers"
- "Call today"
- "No hidden fees"
- "Trade-ins welcome" / "We accept trade-ins"
- "Financing available"
- "Custom design"
- "In stock"
- "NATM Certified" (for US Cargo product listings)
- "Built in Bristol, Indiana" (for US Cargo)
- "Racing" / "karting" / "motorsport" (in brand narrative context)
- First-person "I" (in Jeff's voice)
- Direct action verbs: "Build," "Design," "Call," "Come check us out"

### Words and Phrases to Avoid
- Corporate-speak: "leverage," "synergy," "solutions," "cutting-edge," "world-class"
- Passive voice ("trailers are available" → "we have trailers in stock")
- Generic superlatives with no proof: "best in the industry," "top-rated," "premier"
- Stuffy formality: "we would be pleased to assist you," "please do not hesitate"
- Exaggerated promises: "guaranteed," "we'll beat any price"
- Filler words: "essentially," "basically," "in terms of"
- "Customer" where "you" will do
- Third-person references to Jeff when he's the speaker ("Jeff Hill believes..." → "I believe...")

### Brand-Specific Terminology
- **Jeff Hill Trailer Sales** (full name, no abbreviation in formal use)
- **Jeff Hill Trailer Sales LLC** (legal entity name)
- **DREAM TRAILER** (appears in caps with quotation marks in source material — maintain this treatment in brand-adjacent copy)
- **GVW / GVWR** — standard trailer industry abbreviation, appropriate for product listings
- **NATM Certified** — National Association of Trailer Manufacturers; always spell out first use in non-product contexts
- Trailer dimensions written as **WxL** (e.g., 6x12, 7x14) — standard industry format, acceptable in listings

## Channel-Specific Voice Guidance

### Website Copy
Direct, first-person, action-oriented. Short sentences. CTAs are imperative and simple ("CALL TODAY," "GET YOUR QUOTE TODAY"). Product descriptions shift to technical spec format — this is appropriate. Keep brand narrative sections personal and story-driven; let product pages do the spec work.

### Blog / Long-Form Content
Does not currently exist. If created: write as Jeff speaking to his community — "here's what I've learned about dump trailers," not "a comprehensive guide to dump trailers." Educational but personal. Embed racing/trailer-life experience as examples.

### Facebook
Primary community platform. Facebook Live is in use. Tone here is the most casual and conversational — Jeff as himself, talking to people he knows. Post copy can be very short, direct, and personal. Community questions and engagement are welcomed. Likely mixes product posts with behind-the-scenes and racing-adjacent content.

### Instagram
Active but secondary. Recent posts use racing language ("race into jeff hill trailer sales"). Short captions, product-focused. Casual tone, may use lowercase. Emojis: unknown but likely minimal given overall brand voice.

### Email
No formal email program observed. If built: subject lines should be direct and non-clickbaity ("New 7x14 enclosed trailers just arrived" vs. "You won't believe what just came in"). Body copy matches website voice — personal, brief, with a single clear action.

## What This Voice Is Not

1. **Not corporate.** Jeff Hill Trailer Sales does not speak in third person, does not use brand-speak, and does not hide the owner behind a "we." Any copy that sounds like it came from a marketing agency is wrong for this brand.
2. **Not pushy.** The brand explicitly calls out its opposition to pushy salesmanship. Copy should never use pressure tactics, scarcity manipulation, or aggressive CTAs.
3. **Not overly polished.** This brand is authentic and a little rough around the edges — that's the point. Perfect marketing copy with no personality is the wrong register.
4. **Not generic trailer dealer.** Every other dealer says "we have the best selection at the best prices." Jeff Hill leads with story, relationship, and expertise. Generic positioning language defeats the entire brand.
5. **Not impersonal.** Jeff's phone number is in the logo. Chuck's number is on the homepage. Relationships are the product. Copy that depersonalizes or adds friction to contact is off-brand.

## Competitor Voice Differentiation

Among the five identified competitors (Indiana Trailer Sales, TC Trailers, Trailer Sales of Indiana, Spencer Trailers, Weaver's), none lead with a founder's personal story or a community/family identity as prominently as Jeff Hill. TC Trailers and Spencer Trailers both emphasize family-owned legacy, but at scale — they have hundreds of units and decades of history. Jeff Hill's voice is intimate by necessity and design: you're talking to the guy, not a dealership. That intimacy — the racing story, the first-person copy, the personal cell numbers — is the voice territory no competitor occupies.
