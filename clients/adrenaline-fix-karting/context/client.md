## Their Story

Started in 2007 out of Jim's garage. He identified a gap: drivers who wanted to compete seriously but didn't have the mechanical knowledge or crew to do it right. They'd show up unprepared, make avoidable mistakes, get frustrated. "I figured if I could take that burden off them and just let them focus on driving, we'd be onto something." Grew from there into a proper shop in Columbus, now at New Castle every race weekend. 19 years in.

## What They Do (In Their Words)

> "We're a full-service kart racing team. We build the kart, we prep it, we haul it to the track, we crew it during the race, and we debrief the driver afterward with data. You show up, you drive, you go home. Everything in between is on us."

Two tracks of service:
- **Arrive-and-drive:** Book a slot, everything is ready at the track. Simplest entry point.
- **Full-support clients:** AFK is at every race. Looking at data between sessions, coaching in the paddock, the same crew who built the kart standing in the pit.

First step: talk through what class makes sense, what the driver's goals are (fun on a weekend vs. competitive). Build or spec a kart, get them out to Whiteland or New Castle for a test day.

## Who They Serve (Their Perspective)

> "The person I love working with is someone who's serious about improving. They don't have to be fast yet — they just have to be coachable and committed."

Best clients: adults who found karting later in life — came back to it, or came from another motorsport. They respect the sport, they listen, they show up ready to work. Age range 20s to 50s.

**Explicitly NOT their customer:**
> "We're not a birthday party venue. We don't do yard karts or fun karts — that's not our world, and we're not equipped for it liability-wise. People who want a one-and-done experience just to say they raced aren't really our audience either. We invest a lot in our clients and we want that to go somewhere. If someone just wants to drive around a parking lot, there are other places for that."

**How they currently get clients:** Almost entirely referrals. "We've never really had to market ourselves. The problem is that model has a ceiling — we're only reaching people who already know someone in the karting world. We know there are people out there searching for what we do who just can't find us."

## What Makes Them Different (Their Words)

> "Nineteen years. Nobody in this region has been doing this as long as we have. We've seen every engine class come and go, we've worked with hundreds of drivers at every skill level."

> "We also do everything in-house — we're not farming out engine work or kart builds. When you race with AFK, the same people who built your kart are standing in your pit. That continuity matters."

Client story Jim shared: "I had a client last year who came to me having never been on a real race track. By the end of the season he was podiuming in his class at NCMP. He told me 'I had no idea what I was missing — I thought I was just going to mess around, but now I can't stop.'"

**The misconception he deals with most:**
> "People are always surprised we don't have our own track. They assume because we're a karting company that we run a venue. We're not a venue — we're a team. The distinction matters a lot, and honestly it causes us problems online because Google seems to think we're a go-kart entertainment place."

## Goals for This Engagement

> "New clients who are finding us because they searched for what we do — not because someone handed them our phone number. I want someone in Indianapolis or Columbus to type 'kart racing team' into Google and find us on the first page."

Primary growth target: **Indiana**. Specifically drivers at NCMP coming from Indianapolis, Dayton, Cincinnati who don't have proper team support and currently have no idea AFK exists.

**Relationship to note:** Close partnership with NCMP — "we don't want anything we put out to feel like we're competing with the track itself. We promote NCMP, not replace it."

**Sponsors to include where appropriate:** MAGiK Kart, GreyHound Racing Seats — "they've supported us and we want to return that." *(PaRacing removed — unclear what this is, confirm with Jim before including)*

> "If I'm getting two or three calls a month from people who found us online — people I've never met and who don't know anyone we know — that's success. Right now that number is basically zero."

Baseline: ~0 inbound calls from search. Target: 2–3/month within 6 months.

## Intake Form Responses

**1. Origin story:**
I've been racing karts my whole life. In 2007 I decided there was a real gap in this market — drivers who wanted to compete seriously but didn't have the mechanical knowledge or the crew to do it right. They'd show up to a race weekend unprepared, make avoidable mistakes, and get frustrated. I figured if I could take that burden off them and just let them focus on driving, we'd be onto something. Started out of my garage, built it up from there. Now we've got a proper shop in Columbus and we're at New Castle every race weekend.

**2. How do you describe what you do:**
We're a full-service kart racing team. We build the kart, we prep it, we haul it to the track, we crew it during the race, and we debrief the driver afterward with data. You show up, you drive, you go home. Everything in between is on us.

**3. Typical client engagement:**
Usually someone reaches out because they want to race but don't want to own and maintain equipment. We talk through what class makes sense for them, what their goals are — are they just trying to have fun on a weekend, or are they trying to be competitive? Then we build or spec a kart for them, get them out to Whiteland or New Castle for a test day, and go from there. For our arrive-and-drive customers it's simpler — they book a slot, we have everything ready at the track. For our full-support clients it's a longer relationship. We're at every race, we're looking at their data, we're coaching them between sessions.

**4. Ideal customer:**
The person I love working with is someone who's serious about improving. They don't have to be fast yet — they just have to be coachable and committed. My best clients are adults who found karting later in life, maybe they raced go-karts as a kid and are getting back into it, or they came from another motorsport. They respect the sport, they listen, and they show up ready to work. Age-wise we run the gamut — I've got clients in their 20s and clients in their 50s.

**5. Not your customer:**
We're not a birthday party venue. We don't do yard karts or fun karts — that's not our world, and we're not equipped for it liability-wise. People who want a one-and-done experience just to say they raced aren't really our audience either. We invest a lot in our clients and we want that to go somewhere. If someone just wants to drive around a parking lot, there are other places for that.

**6. Where customers come from:**
Referrals, almost entirely. Someone races with us, they tell a friend, that friend calls us. We've never really had to market ourselves. The problem is that model has a ceiling — we're only reaching people who already know someone in the karting world. We know there are people out there searching for what we do who just can't find us.

**7. What you offer competitors don't:**

We also do everything in-house — we're not farming out engine work or kart builds. When you race with AFK, the same people who built your kart are standing in your pit. That continuity matters.
