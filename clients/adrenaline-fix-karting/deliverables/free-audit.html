Your GBP is currently categorized as "Racing car parts store" — a category for parts retailers. Google uses this to decide what searches to show you for. Right now it's routing you to parts-store queries, not racing team queries. That's the single biggest immediate source of search invisibility.

05 · Key Findings

What We Found

Finding 01

Google Has No Idea What Your Business Does

Every page on conlinss.com — homepage, Arrive & Drive, Race Prep, Trackside, LO-206 — has zero H1 tags and no meta descriptions on service pages. The H1 tag is the primary signal you send Google about what a page covers. Without it, Google is guessing, and it's guessing wrong.

The only structured data Google can read on your entire site is a single WebSite record. It doesn't describe a business, a location, or a service — it's named after your internal Wix project.

Verbatim from your site's source code The only schema record Google can parse is a WebSite type with the name "afk-main" — the Wix project label. There is no LocalBusiness schema, no Service schema, no address, no phone number in structured data. Google is reading nothing about what AFK actually is.
Finding 02

The Wrong Category Is Sending the Wrong Signals

A GBP category isn't just a label — it's how Google decides which searches trigger your listing. "Racing car parts store" places AFK in a parts-retail bucket, not a team/club/motorsport bucket. When someone searches for a karting team or arrive-and-drive option near Columbus or Indianapolis, your listing isn't in the candidate pool.

The category mismatch is visible enough that it's shaping how real customers perceive AFK, as evidenced by your own reviews.

From a 5-star review on your Google listing "IT IS SO LIT IN HERE" — the reviewer describes AFK like an indoor entertainment venue. The category confusion is attracting customers with entirely the wrong expectations about what AFK is.
Finding 03

Three Reviews in 19 Years Erodes Trust on First Contact

When a new prospect finds AFK through search, your GBP is often the first thing they see. Three reviews on a 19-year-old business signals either neglect or low customer satisfaction — neither is the impression you want. With so few reviews, a single negative one has outsized impact.

Your current average is 3.7 stars across 3 reviews. One is a 1-star complaint. With a proper review cadence, that average and volume would look completely different within a month.

Your only 1-star review reads: "Answer your phone." — With only 3 total reviews, this one complaint has the same weight as 33% of your entire review history. Thirty real reviews from real clients would bury it and tell a completely different story.

07 · Immediate Action

One Fix You Can Do Today

Before anything else: fix your GBP. It costs nothing, takes five minutes, and immediately corrects how Google classifies and presents your business.

Fix Your Google Business Profile ⏱ ~5 minutes
  1. 1 Go to business.google.com and sign in with the Google account linked to your GBP listing.
  2. 2 Click Edit Profile → Business category. It currently says "Racing car parts store." Change it. Best available options: "Go-karting venue" (most searchable) or "Motoring club" (more accurate). Either is dramatically better than what's there now.
  3. 3 Still in Edit Profile — find the Website field. It currently points to conlinss.com/contact. Change it to conlinss.com. Anyone clicking from Google should land on your homepage, not a blank contact form.
  4. 4 Save. Google processes updates within 1–3 days. You'll immediately be eligible to appear in karting-related searches instead of parts-store searches.
